Port Stewart’s river floods in the wet, dries up in the dry, and carries metals the community has good reason not to trust. This design never touches the river.

Project context

Port Stewart is a Lama Lama community on Cape York, Queensland. The Yintjingga Aboriginal Corporation (YAC), led by Lama Lama Traditional Owners, is the community partner (Office of the Registrar of Indigenous Corporations, n.d.; North Australian Indigenous Land and Sea Management Alliance, n.d.). The project responds to the 2026 EWB Challenge brief, Design Area 6 (Water Resilience) and Design Opportunity 6.2 (Drinking Water Quality and Treatment) (Engineers Without Borders Australia, 2026a). Through co-design workshops, Lama Lama Elders, EWB and the Centre for Appropriate Technology agreed that rainwater harvesting should be the path forward (Low, 2025).

Existing solutions elsewhere

Think Water. A national franchise that supplies pumps, filtration (including UV) and rainwater tanks to homes, farms and businesses (Irrigation Australia, n.d.-a; n.d.-b).

Kadjina community upgrades. Filtration that removes iron and manganese, with automatic disinfection, in a government-backed $1.9M project (Morse, 2026; Water Corporation, 2026).

Why this is not enough

A cyclone destroyed the original supply in 2006 (Engineers Without Borders Australia, 2019), a 2020 to 2021 bore intake was never built because funding was not secured (Engineers Without Borders Australia, 2021), and the 2024 Ranger Base rainwater system, funded by a $40,000 Yaru Foundation donation, does not serve the homes (Prestipino, 2024; Yaru Water, 2024; Engineers Without Borders Australia, 2026a). A rainwater system commissioned at Port Stewart in August 2025 delivers water that meets the Australian Drinking Water Guidelines, but house connections are still to come (Engineers Without Borders Australia, 2026b). River water is still unsafe and most residents have stopped using it (Engineers Without Borders Australia, 2026a).

Problem description

The Lama Lama community needs safe, reliable drinking water that is available year-round and easy to maintain locally.

Poor water quality, seasonal extremes and limited infrastructure prevent this (Water Source, 2018; Engineers Without Borders Australia, 2026a; Yaru Water, 2024).

What the project aims to achieve

The project aims to design a shared rainwater system for each cluster of about five homes that removes dependence on the river, can be built in stages, needs minimal power and chemicals, and can be looked after with local skills, supporting YAC’s priorities of return to Country and caring for Country (Engineers Without Borders Australia, 2026a).
