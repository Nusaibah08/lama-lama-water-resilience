A shared rainwater system at Port Stewart

A proposed shared rainwater-harvesting system giving the Lama Lama community safe drinking water that does not depend on the Stewart River, using stored wet-season rain.

Design Area(s): 6. Water Resilience   Design Opportunity: 6.2 Drinking Water Quality and Treatment

Keywords: rainwater harvesting, drinking water quality, Lama Lama, Port Stewart, Cape York, slow-sand filtration, solar UV

Proposal Summary

The project aims to address unsafe and unreliable drinking water at Port Stewart through shared rainwater systems, each serving a cluster of about five nearby homes. Rain from household roofs is cleaned, stored in two shared tanks, filtered and treated with solar-powered ultraviolet light, then delivered by gravity to one shared outlet. This is a student proposal and has not been approved by the community or the Yintjingga Aboriginal Corporation (YAC).

Overview of Design

  • The system collects wet-season rain and stores part of it for the dry season, so the community depends less on the Stewart River.
  • Implementation is supported by staged construction, supplier training and Ranger-led maintenance.
  • Components: roof gutters and downpipes, first-flush diverters, two 10,000 L tanks on a raised mound, a slow-sand filter, a solar-powered UV unit with battery, and a shared outlet.
  • Residents of each cluster of about five homes would collect treated water from that cluster’s shared outlet.

Key Considerations

  • It adds to existing sources (the Ranger Base and carted water) and is not a full replacement: in the team’s model the two tanks meet about half of yearly demand and run empty from about May.
  • Maintenance uses skills Rangers already have, and old batteries must leave on the supply truck.
  • The full system’s water quality has not yet been tested. Laboratory testing is required before anyone drinks from it.
  • Next step: consult YAC and the Lama Lama community. Costs still need supplier confirmation.

Proposed Materials and Cost Summary

  • Purchased materials: tanks, gutters and diverters, slow-sand filter, solar UV unit and battery, pipes and fittings. These are catalogue items from Australian suppliers that must be freighted to Port Stewart.
  • Estimated cost (AUD) for one cluster: about $30,400 one-off plus about $2,200 a year for maintenance. Installation and labour are about $4,000 of the one-off cost. Approximate, to be confirmed with suppliers.
